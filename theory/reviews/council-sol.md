# Independent council review

**Recommendation.** Make the next decision on dense KIDA under representative split inputs. Keep rollout-tuned ChebTT and the polynomial chart as panel controls, and narrow the proposed full-panel reruns to a few cases that discriminate sampling, approximation, and propagation. On the target, compare a strong exact integrator, an online local table that reuses solved pairs, and two compact next-state fits on the same data. Give a small full-input residual MLP one CPU trial against a regularized polynomial or reduced-rank linear head. Test a learned representation only where it improves prediction of the next state or detection of failures. Advance to GPU timing only after a candidate meets the 0.01-dex maximum on fresh split chains, passes physical checks, and leaves enough chemistry time to save. R4b establishes the failure of its tested KIDA charts, and its 26 validation-selected panel cases include four test rollout failures. The best case for learning is a measured advantage on the deployment distribution. ## 1. Verdict on section 9, in priority order

1. **Put the deployment experiment before the model search.** Section 9 treats chained mixing as the target regime. The current mixer perturbs each cell with a randomly selected *reference* partner, holds the environment on its prescribed path, and leaves the surrogate's partner errors out of the coupling. No chemistry output feeds back into MHD in this test. Dense KIDA adds four driven environment inputs but uses one 66.7 kyr step, 24 steps and a common initial composition followed by burn-in. Keep both mixing weights as reproducible stress controls. Add a small replay of solver-generated neighbouring-cell inputs with conservative abundance transport, changing environments and actual step sizes, plus a closed chain in which both interacting cells can carry surrogate error. Hold out whole runs, parcels or coupled blocks and a contiguous regime. Check 0.01 dex against the solver on the hybrid's *own* accepted inputs as well as against the solver-only split chain, and check positivity and elemental/charge balances after every projection and floor. Freeze the output mask and explicitly audit species crossing the floor, including material false positives from below the reference floor. If MHD traces are unavailable, a split patch with scripted compression and shielding changes is a useful feasibility test, with applicability limited to that patch. 2. **Replace S4's input-reconstruction veto with a test of predictive sufficiency.** Dense KIDA exhausted the tested PCA charts and affine-to-quartic lifts at $k=1,\ldots,20$ and then tried an unvalidated $k=1$ plus four environmental inputs. The lift routine also returns infinity for over-budget feature sets. The selected cubic has 56 terms and held-out worst/median of 297.79/2.21 dex. All queries fall back. Those results leave the minimum useful input dimension open. Nelson is a direct counterexample to the claim that the lift is always the bottleneck. R4b records a **validated** 12-of-14 species chart with a 0.000164-dex lift check, then a best recorded held-out step-fit maximum of 1.10 dex on the selected affine fit. POLLU reached a 0.00514-dex check at $k=12$, just above the 0.005 cutoff, and reverted to 20. Try predicting the scored next state, or an increment or exact-baseline correction, from full inputs before requiring a bottleneck. Separately test conditional reconstruction of *outputs*, and same-code or nearly same-code input pairs under the same environment, step size and invariant totals. For an exactly shared code a pair of required outputs separated by more than twice the defect budget disproves that deterministic factored head on a common scored component. Near collisions need a bound on the head's allowable slope to make that argument. An input decoder can fail badly on directions the step contracts. It can also reconstruct natural trajectories while missing slow directions activated by transport. Proposition 6.5 gives a lower bound with a suitable set and separation hypothesis, with no assembled KIDA lower count or reason to search only three ranks above it. 4. **Treat the four factors as diagnostics and make propagation an experimental variable.** Proposition 5.1 requires reproduction of a fixed space for the quoted best-approximation inequality. Ridge needs a bias term, rank truncation changes the reproduced space, and solver-label error is amplified by the prediction weights. A pooled 99% quantile of point scores from overlapping folds and different fits supplies an empirical threshold. It has no split-conformal trajectory guarantee on a refit model. Reserve an independent calibration split for the frozen predictor and distinguish an observed maximum from a finite-sample block failure-rate bound and from a tube certificate. TT-cross is a further exception. Even with a fixed pivot the cross construction combines sampled blocks and inverses, so a tensor-grid Chebyshev Lebesgue bound is neither its end-to-end sensitivity nor an acceptance guarantee. Theorem 8 applies to a factored map under its tube hypotheses. Refusal, input-specific projection and normal corrections can break the factorization. For hybrid chains use the full-state defect and split-map propagation, including inherited error on a refused step. Estimate the effect of actual defect directions with paired exact steps or periodic exact checkpoints. Treat the sampled scalar $A_n$ as a stress descriptor. A high worst-direction gain is reason to probe where actual defects point. Stop at a horizon on the measured accuracy–cost trade-off, since a large upper bound can coexist with small realized error. A direction forgotten by the step has $D\Phi\,v\approx0$, hence a large increment derivative on $v$; the docstring's fast-fibre interpretation does not follow. Use the existing table as a control. Reuse fallback labels immediately after scoring and count audits and insertions. A single Taylor record has no informative least-squares leverage gate. A local fit can add leverage to a radius/curvature check once it has rank and independent neighbours. Start with ART or an oblique local fit on visited support with disjoint per-leaf audits and boundary probes. AFINNI's trust distance and ART's routed leaf diagnose geometry, with separate evidence needed for acceptance. Compare affine first and admit quadratic or nonlinear terms only on measured gains. ## 3. Published claims with the weakest transfer to this objective

1. **Headline speed and network size.** NN-36 returns 192-species abundances at requested times and reports an order-of-$10^4$ speed-up, with uncertainties below 0.2 dex. That is a direct-in-time emulator and a different error target. NN-32's 250× and the GPU comparison in the review accompany an offline 77-species RMSE in ppb. The ISAT/DAC hundreds-of-species result and Chemulator's 50,000× likewise warrant checking the mechanism, denominator and outcome before expecting a target speed-up. Test identical split calls against a tuned exact baseline, with fixed accuracy, contiguous batches, and separate single-query and realistic batch timing. Include model loading amortization, transport, data movement, checks, memory and actual fallback costs. [NN-36 primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02387) · [NN-32 primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03874) · [Approaches §6, III-17 and III-21](../APPROACHES.md)

2. **NN-39's uncertainty and stability claims as deployment evidence.** Table 2's FCNN 99th percentiles are 0.38–2.8 dex (roughly 38–280 times the panel budget), and are aggregated log errors. The source actually reports observed maxima in Appendix B (FCNN about 4.33–16.9 dex), while choosing the percentile for tuning. Correct the register's “instead of the maximum” wording accordingly. Its five-member ensemble scores recall at each model's own 99th percentile, and the relative latent robustness comes from mean error under iterated segments of 10 or 3 steps. Table 2 labels the central log metric as a mean, and the latent polynomial is evaluated as a function of time. These details limit transfer to a fixed-step linear latent recurrence or a gate at 0.01 dex. Compare simple support distance and ensemble spread at the *absolute* defect threshold, measure missed exceedances and accepted maxima versus cost-weighted fallback, and re-evaluate through every chemistry step on primordial, Nelson and dense KIDA. Include the always-solver control and report all predeclared splits and seeds. [Janssen et al., §§3–6 and Appendices B–D](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08567v1) · [Literature register NN-39](../LITERATURE.md)

3. **“Coverage alone fixed coupled drift” and broad impossibility claims.** NN-26 changes training through integration, NN-28 changes scaling as well as data coverage, NN-39 conjectures a cause, and NN-73 studies coupled sensitivities. Their evidence supports stress testing coupled feedback. To attribute gains, cross clean versus split training pairs with fixed model versus alternative model at equal labels, and compare teacher-forced defects with model-fed chains. NN-65 gives a worst-case sampling lower bound over target classes containing prescribed ReLU networks. It gives no sample complexity for a particular chemistry map. A power function, numerical variance, random-feature $L^2$ rate, or a PDE operator result in another norm likewise cannot predict this panel's maximum. Treat the literature register's search summaries and abstracts as leads and the models' own held-out tests as evidence limited to their cohorts. ## 5. Minimum target experiment

1. **Establish the baseline and replay.** Use the cached dense trajectories for development, then prepare disjoint groups of short, conservative split chains for added training, calibration and audit. Include ordinary dense cells, compression or shielding transitions, and a held-out environment/duration block inside the intended network range. Compare archived BDF to a tighter reference on a stratified subset, including floor-crossing and expensive steps. Aim for reference disagreement well below 0.01 dex (proposed 0.001 dex on the scored maximum). The KIDA wrapper explicitly densifies an analytic sparse Jacobian and uses BDF. Benchmark a sparse-analytic path or the intended production integrator before quoting acceleration, and log calls, tolerances, time and failures for the exact-only chain. Count unverified regimes as requiring fallback. [KIDA wrapper](../../src/problems/ode/astro.py) · [Integrator](../../src/problems/ode/system.py) · [Dense KIDA driver](../recipe/r3_kida.py)

2. **Do a zero-new-solve viability screen.** On the same grouped prefix use persistence, nearest next state or increment, local linear reuse, the small regularized head and the one compact MLP. Screen held-out *state maxima* against an illustrative local budget of 0.005 dex, with budget sensitivity checked before rollouts. Plot accepted worst versus avoided baseline time. An optimistic selector that knows the reference defects shows how much work each frozen candidate could skip at that local budget on the replay. Its result is a ceiling for the candidate on those inputs. Hybrid trajectories need direct scoring. If no model or growing-table control can accurately cover enough expensive calls even with hindsight, a better gate or GPU cannot rescue those fits on that replay. If one regime accounts for most of the failure, allow one matched-budget round of targeted labels there and repeat on a fresh block. 3. **Run survivors on fresh split chains, then decide whether to use a GPU.** My provisional bar is at least a **5× chemistry speed-up on the target workload** as a reason to expand the study, with accepted one-step defects and the chained maximum within 0.01 dex on every audit block and physical checks passing.     print(name, error.max(), np.median(error), (error <= .01).mean())
