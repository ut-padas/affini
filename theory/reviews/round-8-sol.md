## Findings

1. **Critical — Proposition 8.2 needs a larger Lipschitz domain (§8 and §10 item 3).** The bound compares `Ĝ(π̂(p))` with `Ĝ(χ(π(p)))`. Its hypothesis controls only pairs of points in `χ(π(N))`. The learned code can leave that set by an arbitrarily small amount and incur arbitrarily large error. For example, use the attracting circle from Example 6.8 with zero angular velocity, take the identity embedding as the chart, and set `π̂(p) = (1+h)π(p)`. The exact map on the circle is the identity. The extension `Ĝ(v) = v + A(‖v‖₂−1)e₁` agrees with it there and has Lipschitz constant 1 there. At `p=e₁` the error is `(1+A)h`, while all terms on the displayed right side are independent of `A`. A strict local-chart convention has the same domain gap in each chart. Require a domain containing both images with a bound on the Lipschitz quotient for these particular pairs, or require the learned code to land in the exact chart image. For an atlas, specify the chart-selection rule and check the estimate for every chart that can be selected.

2. **Critical — Corollary 6.3's extension clause gives a false three-term bound (T6).** A common domain alone does not justify the approximation term `sup_S ‖Γ̂−Γτ‖` when `π̂` lands off `S`. For `x′=−x`, take `S={0}`, `N=[−1,1]`, `τ=log 2`, `π=0` and `π̂=1`. Extend `Γτ(x)=x/2` and take `Γ̂(x)=5x` on the same domain. At 0 the error is 5 and the entire displayed right side is 1. Keep the formula when `π̂(N)⊂S`. For the extension, take the approximation supremum over `π̂(N)` or include a Lipschitz constant for `Γ̂` on the pairs reached by `π` and `π̂`. In 6.4 also state the continuity of the fibre projection and carry the remainder into any upper count at a specified tolerance.

3. **Critical — The two rollout conditions still read as a sufficient rule (Demonstration 8.5, “Rollouts,” §G and §11).** The sentence “A rollout holds when” survives the resolution of H6. Both conditions can hold while the rollout exceeds tolerance, even if the Jacobians are evaluated on the surrogate chain. Use the exact identity step, start at 0, take `ε>0` and define `Ψ(y)=y+ε+C h(y/ε)`, with `C>0` and `h(s)=3s²−2s³`. The two-step first-order sum on the exact chain is `2ε`. The surrogate Jacobian is 1 at both its inputs, 0 and `ε`, so every product over those inputs equals the exact product. The actual two-step error is `2ε+C`. Also, the measured training-chain error in G includes nonlinear feedback and measures a different quantity from the first-order defect sum. Call both proposed quantities diagnostics. A sufficient product-based condition needs a tube and a remainder bound, or bounds for the mean Jacobians along the exact-to-surrogate segments, including the defect propagation and tube-closure induction.

4. **Critical — Corollary 4.3 can still be read as a conditional 95% guarantee (T4).** The F1 resolution fixes Proposition 4.1, but 4.3 still says that observing the passes gives the bound “with probability” `1−α`. For a frozen score `e(x)=x` on `[0,1]`, take `N=1`, `α=.05`, `t=.01` and `L=1`. Its reported bound is `.96` while the true supremum is 1. Every passing sample gives a false certificate, and passes occur with probability .01. The correct claim is that **passing and violating the displayed bound** has probability at most `α), for a frozen candidate and score with fresh points. Give that formulation explicitly in 4.3. In 4.1 start the spending schedule at `r=1`, state `0<α,η<1`, and describe small violation sets as potentially missed.

5. **Major — The deployed HIRES map requires another rollout bound (§8 Theorem 8, §G and §10 item 1).** The proof of Theorem 8 is sound for `Ψ=Ĝ∘π̂`. In `g4_hires_recipe.py`, two inputs with the same unclipped code can have different `B(u)(z−D(u))`. In the uncorrected arm, `Projected.predict` still uses the input invariant on the right-hand side of its projection. HIRES varies that invariant across trajectories (`hires_initial`, in `src/problems/ode/chemistry.py`). Therefore a common `Ĝ(u)` across leaves requires the code to determine the invariant. If a fallback is added, its branch belongs to the deployed map too. Separate Theorem 8's certificate for the factored model from the empirical HIRES recipe. Condition on the invariant or include it in the code for the uncorrected map. For the correction and intended splitting, certify the composite full-state or coupled latent-and-residual update with a specified input tube and propagation bound. If the projection takes a positivity floor, verify conservation and positivity on that domain as well.

6. **Major — Nearest singularities can recede with the step (§T1 Proposition 1.5 and §11).** The nesting of real-time complex existence domains is correct. The general singularity-monotonicity question has a negative answer. Use the real polynomial planar system obtained from `w′=iw+w²` with `w=x+iy`. In the complexification, `w` and `x−iy` are independent linear coordinates obeying conjugate equations. The first flow component is `e^{it}w₀/[1−w₀(e^{it}−1)/i]`. A small real box with `|w₀|<1/2` admits solutions through one period. At `t=π` the complex time map has a finite pole. At `t=2π` the time map is the identity on its existence domain and continues to an entire map. Restrict the monotonicity question to the Brusselator or a stated class. Nesting controls the radii supported by existence alone. Bounds on the size of the map on those ellipses can improve, as for a stable linear flow.

7. **Major — Proposition 2.3 leaves both its defect budget and isometric norm implicit (T2).** The formula for the sum is exact after adding the remainder. The stated bound uses `‖e_k‖≤kε` and norm preservation by the powers of `U`. Orthogonality gives the latter in Euclidean norm, and the former follows from `‖δ(y_k)‖≤ε` on all deployed inputs. A bound only on the exact inputs supplies no such estimate. State the Euclidean or a `U`-invariant norm, the defect budget along the surrogate chain and the Lipschitz bound for the paired exact and surrogate inputs. For the phase claim, specify a finite Fourier expansion of the **pulled-back** sequence and zero discrete orbit mean, or assume nonresonance of its nonzero Fourier modes.

8. **Major — Corollary 3.2's dimension-free conclusion needs uniform dynamical and box scales (T3).** Even with one incoming edge, `ℓΔτ=1` and unit half-widths, the family `x₁′=d x₁+x₂`, with the other coordinates stationary and `τ=1`, has radius-zero freezing error `(e^d−1)/d`. A direct version of the result uses `h*=max_j h_j` and sums entries of each `L^k` over all omitted inputs. The row sum is at most `(ℓΔ)^k` and every omitted term has `k>r`. This gives `h* e^{ντ} Σ_{k>r}(ℓΔτ)^k/k!`, uniform when those factors are uniform. No distance-shell cardinality assumption is needed for that bound. For a dimension-independent **rank** bound, also specify a uniform window size and the assumed interpolation degree.

9. **Major — The compound estimate does not predict the PCA lift count (Demonstration 8.4 and §10 item 2).** Theorem 6.6 controls `σ_m(DΦ)` in a specified norm. It gives no bound for the finite displacement made by projecting a visited state onto its PCA subspace. Even with a constant Jacobian and a fast spectral gap, the discarded PCA direction can be a slow direction if the data have small variance along it. The four Fisher–KPP measurements are useful evidence for these sampled ensembles. The committed quarter-tolerance counts are 7, 8, 8, 8 at `d=10,20,40,80`. `reduced_dims.py` also predicted a count equal to the visited dimension for HIRES and POLLU. Its affine result is 7 on HIRES versus the estimated dimension 3, and 16 on POLLU versus the later estimate of about 7. Report the literal prediction outcomes, call the ladder trend empirical, and use a uniform bound for `DΦ` along the actual lift segments to connect the PCA residual to the error. In 6.6, write the mesh convention and positive diffusion coefficient explicitly. The unit-interval cell-centred Neumann eigenvalues `4n² sin²((i−1)π/(2n))` give the stated lower bound and hence an explicit bound on `σ_m` by taking the product's `m`th root.

10. **Major — Section G conflates derivative observations with verified constants and with a Taylor error law (§G opening, recipe and §11).** An oracle Jacobian at a point measures or approximates a *local* derivative of the exact step. Theorem 8 needs uniform gains of the fitted map and uniform value defects. Its gains can be bounded from the fitted coefficients without querying oracle derivatives. Proposition 6.5 also needs a Hessian bound over a ball, and 6.7(a) concerns a constant vector-field Jacobian. For the correction, let `n=z−D(u)`, `J=DΦ`, and bound the Hessian by `K` on the segments from `D(u)` to `z`. Before projection the one-step error is at most
    `‖G(u)−Φ(D(u))‖ + ‖B(u)−J(D(u))‖‖n‖ + K‖n‖²/2`.
    Only the last term is quadratic. A finite-range slope near one cannot identify the dominant term or establish a first-order asymptotic. The on-trajectory derivative rows use `J(X)D′(u)` and differ from the derivative of `Φ∘D` by at most `K‖X−D(u)‖‖D′(u)‖` under the same segment hypothesis, plus numerical errors. Give this budget, include the effect of the deployed projection and clipping, and label the slope as an observation. Running the exact pair with the *same* test points and plotting error versus residual would distinguish the terms better than comparing maxima at two kick sizes.

## Overall assessment

Theorem 8 is a sound starting point for a deterministic finite-horizon certificate on a specified family of exact inputs for a factored surrogate. The antipodal obstruction, the local spectral bounds and the distributional audit offer complementary tools. The memo in its current form does not yet supply a sound foundation for the **deployed corrected or split** scheme. The three changes that matter most are:

1. Repair the domains of the off-set estimates and state a rollout theorem for the actual deployed map on a closed, explicitly specified split-input tube.
2. Complete one worst-case certificate with independently validated reference defects and propagation bounds, and use the training-chain score only as a selection diagnostic.
3. Reconcile the tagged prediction verdicts, rerun the off-trajectory comparison with separate value and derivative arms, and give each headline result a matched cohort and immutable provenance.
