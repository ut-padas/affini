## 2. Versatile tensor trains

**Guarantee first.** For a frozen representation $\phi(x)$, a linear readout $S$ has label weights $\ell(x)$. For any comparator $q$ in a fixed space, the raw prediction error is bounded by

$$
\|f(x)-q(x)\| + \sum_i |\ell_i(x)|\,\|f(x_i)-q(x_i)\| + \|q(x)-Sq(x)\| + \sum_i |\ell_i(x)| b_i ,
$$

where $b_i$ bounds reference-label errors, and the last two terms are the reproduction defect and label uncertainty. In full-rank unregularized LS the reproduction term vanishes. For vector outputs this holds in any fixed norm applied consistently. For component-dependent weights use separate rows or an operator-norm bound. A $\sqrt N\|\ell\|_2$ calculation measures amplification. A gate using it certifies a defect only when the approximation, bias and reference terms are independently bounded. For TT-ALS a core is linear conditional on the other cores. A last-sweep per-core score measures sensitivity of that update with the environments frozen. It can miss directions absent from the learned features and cannot bound the end-to-end map from labels to fitted cores. Pivoting and inversion in TT-cross also change the operator. An independent net with a residual modulus can audit the final model regardless of training. These conclusions follow by adding and subtracting $q$ and $Sq$. [Memo T4–T5](../MEMO.md) · [TT environments](../../src/schemes/chebtt/model.py) · [TT-cross](../../src/schemes/chebtt/train.py)

Rank the synthesis candidates **T-a > T-b > T-c > T-e > T-d**, with a **frozen TT feature map and common linear head** as the first implementation. Fit the input cores on discovery data only, freeze the latent product through the last input core, and use an independent, reasonably conditioned readout fit. Start with the same train/calibration/audit partitions, masks and labels for TT, a total-degree chart and a hub or additive/stencil head. Count unique full-vector oracle calls and measure bytes, contraction cost, label cost, accepted maxima and coupled rollout. Pre-register 0.01 for step maps, a fixed matched tolerance for functions, and a minimum 2× change in labels or total prediction-and-gate time as the criterion to replace a passing control. ## 3. When to share outputs

1. **Separate output compression, shared input directions and local dependence.** Stack centered/scaled targets (successors, increments and corrections to the same cheap baseline) and inspect singular tails. For Jacobians, stack $J(x)$ vertically to test a common input subspace and horizontally to test a common output tangent space. A low pointwise rank can coexist with a large global span. The wave pair has rank-one Jacobians and a common input direction, while the image has two-dimensional affine span. Rosenbrock gradient tests the reverse engineering choice of shared stencils with full output rank. The hub and ChebTT-M share input coordinates/groups. Their benefit need not imply output compression. Rank diagnostics use scaled physical errors and max component tails, with held covariates carried explicitly. [Vector function definitions](../../src/problems/vector_fields.py) · [f3 records](../functions/data/f3_vector.json) · [Schemes](../../docs/SCHEMES.md)
2. **Budget the decoder before predicting codes.** For $f(x)=b(x)+D a(x)+r(x)$, test the error with oracle codes first. A fixed linear decoder gives $\|f-\hat f\|_\infty\le \|r\|_\infty+\|D\|_{\infty\leftarrow\infty}\|a-\hat a\|_\infty$. Apply row weights or allocate budgets per output for a sharper bound. For a nonlinear decoder use a verified paired Lipschitz constant over both actual codes. On a fixed domain include postprocessing via a bound on its Lipschitz gain and its action on the exact output. To certify physical invariants, an affine decoder in the null space of an **exact** law with baseline on the correct leaf conserves them before floors. Test positivity and conservation on the deployed output. Low-dimensional geometry in log outputs does not imply conservation in abundance space. Under masked scoring require a uniform bound over components that can enter the score on the certified region, or audit floor crossings and tighten the bound near them. Give a decoder a quarter of the allotted one-step defect budget in the first oracle screen. [Memo T5 and §8](../MEMO.md) · [Projection](../../src/benchmark/step_maps.py) · [KIDA mask](../panel/p5_kida_screen.py)
3. **Prefer shared design with a compact head when it earns its rank.** A grouped reduced-rank regression on weighted outputs is the first predictive test, with an independent readout calibration if the factors are learned. Try both masked minimax decoder fitting and Euclidean PCA as controls. For ordinary TT the cost with a terminal output core is roughly $\sum_k(n_k+1)r_{k-1}r_k+r_d m$ multiply-adds per query. Compare the actual sum for independent trains, which already share vector oracle calls and can share basis evaluation. The grouped head wins only when the union of input bond spaces stays cheap. Include strict errors and full-state evolution even when the scored subset is small. In my post hoc p5 split check, global PCA of increments at rank 64, supplied with *true* held-out codes, gives 0.149 dex key maximum and 39.8% key coverage at 0.01. Successor PCA gives 0.206 dex and 19.1%. At rank 128 the increment maxima are 0.105 key and 0.263 strict. This rules out those specific compression budgets for this Euclidean decoder. It leaves near-minimax, local and nonlinear decoders open, and it motivates spending little on a global low-rank KIDA head before testing its reconstruction. ## 4. Held-out maxima and data design

**Diagnose the worst rows in this order.** First compare the same code receiving different inputs that splitting can produce, holding duration, external inputs and invariant totals fixed or conditioning on them explicitly. If two inputs have the same code and require outputs separated by more than twice the defect budget on a common scored coordinate, a deterministic factored predictor cannot meet both budgets. For near collisions, subtract an allowed change of the head (its Lipschitz constant times the code separation). Next compare a well-conditioned fit on strong local coverage against independent normal/along-tube probes to estimate the approximation floor. Then stratify amplification, clipping, projection, reference error and floor-reactivation by start, later and split input. A single large training-to-test ratio cannot distinguish these mechanisms. With a frozen linear space, the error decomposition in §2 supplies the tests: conditioning cannot fix the comparator error or ridge bias, and no sample conditioning term bounds the future chain without propagation. Rank-deficient pseudoinverse fits need a reproduced subspace or an explicit discarded-direction term. A calibrated maximum of held-out residual ratios is no upper bound on best uniform approximation error. ## 5. Build on a tube when the deployment domain supports it

1. **Define feasible patches and two different radii.** On each patch use a chart $u$, a feasible lift $D(u)$, and a normal displacement $v$, measuring errors in a fixed, scaled state norm. Carry external inputs, step duration and any varying invariant totals in $u$ or explicitly condition on them. Set $r$ for $\|x-D(u)\|$ and keep an interior reserve for deviation of the hybrid from reference inputs. Reach/injectivity is needed if using unique closest points or intrinsic normal coordinates. An explicit feasible lift and bounded residual are sufficient for the following direct comparison even where nearest projection is nonunique. The gate can certify membership in the stated patch with rigorous chart and residual checks. It cannot establish that a trace-based decoder spans the relevant states. Never generate a full rectangular grid in learned coordinates until every designed point has a feasible lift. [Memo §8](../MEMO.md) · [Current chart and residual](../panel/generic2.py)
2. **Budget tangential and normal error separately.** On a patch with $x=D(u)+v$, for exact step $\Phi$ and predictor $G(u)$, the raw one-step defect is at most
   $$
   E_{\rm lift}(u)+L_\perp(u)\|v\|,
   \qquad E_{\rm lift}(u)=\|G(u)-\Phi(D(u))\|.
   $$
   Here $L_\perp$ bounds **paired** exact-map differences or the normal derivative along admissible lift segments. With an added $B(u)v$, if $D\Phi$ is $M$-Lipschitz along those segments, replace the normal term by $\|(D\Phi(D(u))-B(u))v\|+M\|v\|^2/2$. Even if the step contracts normal directions strongly, a small normal-to-tangent displacement can matter for later dynamics. Proposition 8.2 is a different conditional comparison with the ideal code $\chi\pi(x)$. It requires both a bound between the ideal and learned code (e.g. $\|\hat\pi-\chi\pi\|\le a+\kappa r$ on the patch) and a Lipschitz constant for $\hat G$ valid **on the paired codes**, along with the stable-fibre remainder. Substituting the geometric radius for code mismatch is unjustified without such an inequality. With a gate, floor, projection or blend, bound defects of the deployed output, or add bounds for those transformations explicitly. [Memo §8, Proposition 8.2 and Theorem 8 scope](../MEMO.md) · [Library projection](../../src/benchmark/step_maps.py)
3. **Design along feasible tangents and across the normals actually used.** Use nested Chebyshev points in a certified coordinate rectangle, on the base and on few bounded normal fibres, where their interpolation weights have a tensor Lebesgue bound. High dimension still multiplies the per-axis constants and node counts. On irregular charts choose feasible scattered samples to control amplification on the target patches. Use a chart polynomial or frozen TT features for the tangential fit and a small affine-in-normal correction where split probes show it is needed. Compare a phase/regime patch atlas when a single lift is ill-conditioned. Place labels along mixed neighbour difference directions and a few independent normal stress directions, reserving unseen signs and neighbour pairs for audit. Neither one-step residuals on the fitted rows nor low ALS rank controls between-node defects. For a certificate use valid bounds on approximation and label error as in §2 or, in a norm covering the scored components, a verified residual net with fill distance $h$, modulus $L_\delta$, and label error $b$, giving $\max_i e_i+L_\delta h+b$. Conditional membership alone does not imply an accuracy bound. [Memo T4–T5 and §8](../MEMO.md)
4. **Close the tube for the split map.** Let the exact coupled update be $F_j=\Phi_j\circ T_j$, including the environment update and any feedback in the state. Compare the exact and hybrid *full* states in a fixed norm. While the reference and hybrid inputs lie in the certified region, write
   $$
   E_{j+1}\le K_j E_j+d_j+\eta_j .
   $$
   $K_j$ bounds the paired exact composite difference, $d_j$ bounds the hybrid's post-transport own-input defect and $\eta_j$ budgets numerical reference/fallback errors as needed. An exact fallback sets the surrogate's $d_j$ to zero at that cell and preserves inherited error. Unroll with the products of $K_j$ or use verified directional/block gains and remainders. A valid induction also requires the propagated deviation **after the next transport** to fit the interior reserve; otherwise change the domain or policy. In a nonexpansive scalar model for 170 steps, a uniform budget with no other error is $0.01/170$, not 0.005. Gains can make the requirement looser or tighter. Empirical derivative products along a few exact chains or observed error cancellation are diagnostic. Theorem 8 for an actually factored autonomous map does not apply unchanged to these split/fallback branches. [Memo E, T2 and §8](../MEMO.md) · [p6 transport](../panel/p6_column.py) · [HIRES horizon](../../src/problems/ode/step_maps.py)

## 6. A gate that transfers

- **Freeze geometry first, calibrate the conjunction as deployed.** Fit/chart, choose geometry and physical checks on discovery data, then evaluate conditional risk only in that feasible subset. Compute residual, support, finite fit, physical and solver eligibility exactly as they will be applied. Treat empty calibration strata and nonfinite cases as explicit refusal. Resolve tied scores conservatively by group. The p5/p7 prefix-threshold code admits ties using “≤” and does not group the first bad score's ties. R4d filters fold calibration by residual but pools scores and thresholds from folds with other models and geometries. Fit the deployed predictor once and reserve independent blocks for its actual calibration. Use disjoint policy-selection blocks with exact labels on both teacher-forced and model-fed states. Test the final frozen policy on an outer block split. A same-budget support-only rule is an important control. 100% refusal is an all-exact outcome. Count every acceptance, reference exception and cap in reporting. [p5 calibration](../panel/p5_kida_screen.py) · [p7 calibration](../panel/p7_online.py) · [R4d and final refit](../panel/generic3.py)
- **Match guarantees to evidence.** Record analytic membership, deterministic defect bounds where a verified net or enclosure is possible, and empirical block maxima as different claims. A finite exchangeable-block guarantee can use the split-conformal order statistic $k=\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$ of whole-block scores for a fixed policy, with $+\infty$ if $k>n$. The test block's rank among $n+1$ exchangeable blocks gives marginal coverage, counting ties conservatively. This does not bound the absolute maximum, guarantee coverage in a shifted regime, or guarantee the error of a policy repeatedly tuned on those same blocks. A score can be the maximum over cells and time of accepted own-input defects and hybrid/reference errors normalized by their respective budgets, with physical violations or failures assigned infinity. Use a conformal quantile at most one as a policy qualification rule, with the score and policy frozen before calibration, or use the memo's fixed-policy pass/fail audit. The rank argument states marginal coverage of the resulting score threshold. It supplies no conditional probability guarantee given qualification. ## 8. Conservation-law fix and affected results

**Declare laws or absence of laws.** A linear law satisfies $WF(y;\xi)=0$ for all states and admissible held inputs $\xi$ on its stated domain. For Burgers the periodic flux and second difference telescope, so the constant row is exact. For Fisher and Allen–Cahn, independent polynomial terms in each coordinate force zero coefficients in a putative $W$. For Brusselator the reaction term forces matching weights for $u_i,v_i$ via $u_i^2v_i$ and the net $A-u_i$ term then forces every weight to zero. Use stoichiometric identities for chemistry. This gives documented rows or a documented empty set. Check the declaration dimensions, held columns, independence and analytic identities. The declared-law QR needs numerical rank handling if arbitrary dependent rows can reach it. Use unknown-law inference as a diagnostic. Independently scaled admissible RHS probes away from the collapsed traces, including varied environments, can falsify candidates, and polynomial coefficient identities can verify them where available. Sampling with an SVD cutoff alone cannot prove a universal identity, and extremely disparate rates can still hide weak directions. Avoid enforcing unknown rows based only on that test. **Trace dependencies before rerunning.**

- In `chebtt_roll`, projection is used **in the stopping check** and the function returns the raw model. Spurious laws can change whether a round stops, tolerances, evaluations, fit time and every downstream raw and projected score. The three-round cap can also return a model even when the training check never passes. The recorded `one_step_tol` can be the next requested tolerance on cap exhaustion. This affects p4 inclusion and p6 construction. In `run`, other methods' raw training/rollout scores have no projection dependency merely because the projected fields are recorded beside them. Their invariant count and projected metrics are affected where $W$ changes. `rollout` itself steps the policy it receives. Exact trajectory labels and raw dynamics estimates are independent of this projection. A correction can change a method's usability in both directions. p6 compares per-cell totals of two different simulations. Report pre/post-chemistry balance in physical units with a physical scale, along with column-wide totals, and post-floor positivity. Comparing projected exact labels to their unprojected values isolates a projection error independently of the surrogate.
