# Recipe round 1 review

## Diagnosis

The training-chain rule accepts severe generalisation failures. Whole-trajectory holdout catches several of them and selects much better polynomials, but a single 80/20 split is also sensitive to which trajectories set the chart bounds. The records show unstable fits **inside** the chart box and saturation at its faces. Memo §G and the mixing tests also implicate missing dependence on directions the chart drops. The right intervention depends on which effect dominates.

I read the finished per-case records and log while R2 continued. The checks below load existing trajectory arrays directly through `np.load(STORE.data(name, "trajectories.npz"))`, bypassing the cache-generating `data()` function. They use CPU, MPI off, two OpenMP and BLAS threads, and no writes to the run directories. Unless specified, the degree check holds the recorded $k$ fixed, calls `heldout_degrees` with its implemented split (`seed=0`, permutation seed 2) and refits the selected degree on all training trajectories. The test column is an audit of that choice. These are single-split diagnostics. Each reported exploratory arm grouping ran in under 18 seconds.

- **High-degree instability is visible before rollout feedback.** The worst one-step errors for these six failures are on the first step. At the R2 degrees, the largest one-step errors on inputs *inside* the padded chart box are 1.516, 2.374, 1.234 and 1.434 on the four small ladder problems in table order. Only 1, 3, 2 and 3 exact test inputs out of 1,600 lie outside. The full-training design has numerical rank 451/455 on Fisher d3, 793/1,820 on Brusselator d4, 1,301/1,820 on Lorenz d4 and 1,390/2,002 on Allen–Cahn d5 using the default least-squares cutoff. A small residual with that design says little about the polynomial at a fresh initial state. Allen–Cahn amplifies the initial defect in the chain, but already incurs 2.26 at step one.
- **The initial law matters, and $k$ is an operational lift result.** Lorenz starts in a full box. Fisher–KPP, Allen–Cahn and Brusselator use smooth random fields normalised by their maximum absolute value. Their initial laws have structure and may lie on faces, even when the pooled trajectory cloud spans every PCA direction. CHEMAKZO starts in a three-parameter family and has a burn-in. Its degree-4 lift misses the 0.005 cutoff at $k=4$ by about 0.00050, so the selected $k=5$ does very little to establish intrinsic dimension. Robertson starts in a varying initial layer on a fixed mass leaf, and its largest error is at that layer. The one-coordinate, post-initial-layer example in Memo §8.3 covers a narrower task. HIRES starts with two varying quantities, burns in for five time units and samples a thin curved family over time. The memo's three-coordinate result is consistent with the records. The full-training unprojected degree-10 fit scores 4.70 on the same chains on which the projected fit scores 1.48e-4. The invariant is part of the successful deployment. Brusselator d2, Fisher d2 and Allen–Cahn d2 also succeed on ordinary rollouts with $k=d$, so reduction is a cost and conditioning opportunity, and full dimension alone does not imply failure. In the existing interface, `Model.predict` returns an increment, while the regression target is the next state. This is intentional. For an unclipped full affine chart with identifiable unregularised coefficients and $q\ge1$, the identity lies in the polynomial space, so subtracting it before fitting and adding it back changes no fitted function in exact arithmetic. Rank truncation, ridge, clipping and partial charts break that equivalence. A partial-chart fit to $z'-z$ added to the full input has identity response in dropped directions, which a stiff map can damp strongly. A safer residual baseline in that regime is $D(u)$ or a fitted linear flow, optionally with a measured normal correction. In a small degree-2–7 grid, an identity skip with no ridge worsened Fisher d3's clamped test chain from 0.0892 to 0.287 at degree 5, and improved Lorenz d4's degree-3 result from 0.0285 to 0.0171, still outside tolerance. Removing clipping made the two target choices agree to about 1.5e-12 in the selected degree-7 Lorenz arm. Memo §8's Robertson checks show the same limitation of the identity skip on a reduced chart.

## Ranked fixes and decisive tests

A fallback improves system accuracy on refused queries and its cost and coverage belong in the result.

**Verified literature.** Cohen and Migliorati prove stability/accuracy results in a prescribed $L^2$ measure for independently sampled, appropriately weighted least squares, with sample counts proportional to dimension up to a logarithm. That motivates leverage or Christoffel sampling of *new* oracle designs. The assumptions and result do not turn correlated trajectory rows into a maximum-rollout bound. Guo, Narayan and Zhou demonstrate sparse recovery with preconditioned gradient-enhanced regression and give a reason to scale the derivative blocks before judging them. Pope's ISAT tabulates the region the calculation actually accesses, uses local derivatives and acceptance regions, and integrates when coverage is missing. It is the closest precedent for the mixing task. Its reported combustion results motivate an experiment under this recipe's own rollout and cost metrics. [Cohen and Migliorati](https://smai-jcm.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/smai-jcm.24/) · [Guo, Narayan and Zhou](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08837) · [Pope](https://tcg.mae.cornell.edu/pubs/Pope_CTM_97.pdf)

## Next experiment

Three cases. Use cached arrays read-only, CPU/MPI off, two BLAS and OpenMP threads, and cap each process at 150 seconds. Use fixed trajectory folds and consistent raw-truth scoring. Tune on the training folds, audit once on the existing tests, and show first-step and complete-chain errors, validation spread, fallbacks and solver counts. Treat these test paths as paired controls for the repair. Reserve fresh initial and mixing seeds for an independent accuracy claim. The caps below keep the additional oracle work small.

1. **Lorenz-96 d4 — isolate selection, input support and regularisation.** Sweep degrees 3–7 without ridge and with a short ridge grid, validating the clamped model, a modest padded range followed by refusal, and the identical polynomial evaluated through that margin. Fit on three 80/20 trajectory splits and use the smallest degree clearing tolerance across them. A successful repair holds 0.01 on all 40 chains with negligible fallback. The original clamp and degree-12 fit provide controls. No mixed oracle is needed for this case.
2. **Fisher–KPP d10 — check the lift before buying data.** On a clean fold take 40 stratified sites, compare affine and quartic lifts for $k=6,7,9$, and fit degrees 2–4 on the viable charts. Compare grouped chain error first. If that misses, spend 128 one-step solves on new starts sampled from the actual initial law. Compare to 128 space-filling starts on the same admissible initial family, holding the chart and downstream selection protocol fixed. Chart-selection solves and the augmentation budget are separate. Success on neither arm warrants the existing ambient fallback, and a successful lift without a successful chain still fails the recipe gate.
3. **HIRES — preserve the control and measure the mixing failure.** Hold $k=3$, degrees 9–10 and projection fixed. Repeat the clean-fold and full-fit ordinary score, recording exact chart misses, decoder residuals and projection failures. In mixing use eight fixed paths for the full 170-step horizon and the same partner schedule in each arm, referenced to the solver with the same mixing. Compare plain, support gate plus solver, and a local exact-Jacobian correction on a separate bank of at most 16 first-mix/late-mix sites to diagnose whether the normal term is worth fitting. Use a paired holdout to set the gate and report accepted error as well as solver share. A gate that needs nearly every solve still preserves accuracy but provides little acceleration. Full 40-path mixing can wait until an arm survives this check.
