## 2. How to cut refusal

**The obligation that survives every change.** Let `F_k` be the exact split substep, including driver feedback where present, and let `H_k` be the deployed update, including any output projection, casts and numerical fallback. On a verified reachable tube use a fixed componentwise or per-cell enclosure `u_k` of the full error and a nonnegative gain matrix `A_k` for `F_k`. If `d_k` bounds the own-input defect `H_k(x) − F_k(x)` in the same coordinates, adding and subtracting `F_k(x)` gives `u_{k+1} ≤ A_k u_k + d_k`. An exact fallback at the hybrid input sets its local term to zero in the mathematical limit and still carries `A_k u_k`. A numerical fallback has its own defect allowance. Prove headroom for fallback before committing steps, or checkpoint and replay if inherited error can exhaust it. For a changing abundance mask, bound the full coupled state and then restrict the score, or prove an active union that includes possible crossings and bounds any omitted feedback. For static scalar and vector functions use the same local defect bound with no rollout account. The current summed typical charge has none of those propagation guarantees. For the empirical arm, every proposed change produces a new policy to freeze and audit. The deployed sketch `S` gives `mean_train(b_sketch²) = ||S||_F²`, whose expectation is `r` for this normalization. For the fixed draw the bound on the fitted-row fraction over the cap is `min(1, ||S||_F²/(9r))`, with no implication for closed input coverage. The exact out-of-span statistic vanishes for a full-rank design. A computed `Q S'` from subtracting two nearly identical matrices can leave a tiny, precision-dependent cap in that case. Inspect the leverage, span and decoder-offset caps separately, with overlaps, along with magnitude/validity, envelope and account-only refusals. Fix the fit and test single-cap and joint changes on development blocks before refitting. For any enlarged patch, the LP's sampled inequalities survive at their original sample points subject to numerical error. To make a bound on the enlargement, obtain fresh labels and either a verified residual remainder or an independent closed-policy audit. Even exact leverage is a multiplier on a separately established retained-space approximation error, including bias in discarded directions. Blindly raising beta or disabling the offset cap supplies neither. Compare an exact statistic, the frozen sketch, and a span statistic set identically to zero only when the retained rank equals the feature count. Treat the latter as a policy change and recalibrate. ## 3. GPU inference and batching

1. **Use measured fallback time and the fastest qualifying denominator.** For serial stages let `T_0` be total time for the fastest qualifying exact-only simulation and `T_h = T_common + T_eval + T_route + T_fallback + T_other` for the hybrid. Solve count `rho` yields `S ≤ 1/rho` only in the equal per-call cost, serial-work model with a consistent denominator and unchanged fallback cost. Even then, a common simulation stage reduces total achievable speed. With variable solve cost on identical inputs, measure the fraction of solver time on refused states and cost per refused state divided by average cost. In real paired runs the hybrid changes the inputs and the fallback batch size, so measure its actual contribution to the critical path. If work overlaps, use a synchronized timeline and end-to-end wall time. To claim 2× require `T_h ≤ T_0/2`. Under the serial-cost approximation, the budget left for everything besides fallback is `T_0/2 − T_fallback`; a nonpositive budget rules out evaluator optimization as a route to 2×. CHEMAKZO and POLLU have no such budget at the observed p17 refusal rates under its cost model. Fisher d10 does. At unsorted `D=0.05`, its p15 mean is 83.63 µs, refusal 3.725%, and p17 evaluator 41.64 µs/cell. The corresponding evaluator-plus-dispatch budget is 38.70 µs/cell before common work. This is a CPU proxy, useful for prioritizing a timing experiment. 1. **Use independent work first.** Shard oracle labels by stable trajectory or simulation-block IDs, schedule each blocked fold and each candidate or local patch on one GPU, and parallelize independent calibration modes and evaluation blocks. Keep full closed blocks intact until they are too large for one device, and keep the time loop local to each. Aggregate small summaries, maximum errors, flags and work counts. The degree search and lambda search both have stopping logic, so execute a small frontier of independent jobs and preserve the sequential selection rule. Assigning every degree or lambda speculatively spends extra oracle and fit time. ART/AFINNI scatter independent work and collect results and have work stealing for variable cost, a closer model for this first stage than synchronizing a global gradient each iteration. Test fixed total jobs and per-GPU fixed workloads on 1, 2 and 4 GPUs, and include data loading, idle time and amortized GPU-hours. [Selection loop](../panel/generic4.py#L563) · [Lambda loop](../panel/generic4.py#L691) · [Engine scatter/merge](../../src/engine/driver.py#L154) · [Work-stealing implementation](../../src/engine/work_stealing.py)

2. **Shard one least-squares fit by rows only when it is a bottleneck.** With common frozen features and `A_i,Y_i` local, a Gram method all-reduces `sum A_i^T A_i` and `sum A_i^T Y_i`, payload `p(p+1)/2 + p o` doubles if packed. It squares the condition number. Singular values at `1e-10` of the largest become Gram eigenvalues at `1e-20` of the largest, beneath a generic FP64 Gram rounding scale. So a Gram all-reduce cannot in general recover the present rank decision or gate factors. Reserve it for an independently conditioned, numerically qualified arm, with QR/SVD for the rest. For tall-skinny QR, locally factor `A_i=Q_i R_i` and keep `C_i=Q_i^T Y_i`. Merge by QR on stacked `R_i` and apply the same orthogonal transform to stacked `C_i`, including rank-deficient shards with consistent unpivoted feature order. Discarded parts of the right-hand side contribute a coefficient-independent residual. At the root take the SVD of `R`, solve the truncated fit from its left factors and `C`, and build `M=V_r diag(1/s_r)` using global `N` for leverage. This is the same estimator in exact arithmetic. Payload is the same order per merge, about 2.43 MiB in FP64 for `p=792,o=5`, and a balanced tree gives a logarithmic number of merges on the critical path. It pays about `O(Np²/G)` local work for `G` devices, `O(p³)` work per triangular merge and a root SVD. As `N/G` approaches `p`, merge, SVD, synchronization and memory traffic can erase the benefit. The products, rows and decoder need the same global input scaling and chart. Compute global extrema and means and use a similarly stable factorization for the chart if necessary.    Do not let MPI rank count choose the reduction order if invariant results are required. Use fixed logical row chunks and a fixed merge tree mapped to ranks, or qualify a stated tolerance with conservative rank/gate treatment. Keep folds and seeds fixed under repartition, and test condition numbers, singular values near the cutoff, model bytes, maximum defects and hybrid decisions at 1/2/4 devices. Compare wall time to independent-fit scheduling and to the best single-device hybrid trainer at matched models. Investigate TSQR only when a fit misses device memory or dominates total offline time. 3. **Replicate the model and keep cell ownership for inference.** Conditional on its input and local history, the map itself has no cross-cell work. Partition by the simulation's existing mesh ownership and keep predictor, gate and account state resident on each device. Transfer halo values and the required error enclosure on split stages, and carry account state with a migrated cell. Exact fallbacks still make the slowest partition matter at the split-step barrier. Compact per rank and redistribute expensive fallbacks only if saved tail time beats transfers and return synchronization. The library's public `predict` explicitly scatters from rank 0 and gathers there, an API for a different ownership model. Avoid this central staging in a GPU simulation loop. Measure weak scaling at fixed local cells and strong scaling at fixed global cells. Expect strong scaling to stall near the local batch crossover or when one rank gets most refusals. For large atlases compare model replication against selective placement, charging remote lookups and model-cache misses to hybrid time. ## 6. What to measure in inference

1. **Maximum error, useful coverage and failure accounting.** Record the full scored maximum and location per independent block and by split stage, the same-input signed defect and inherited contribution at peaks, including peaks on fallback, and the margin to all caps. Separately record invalid states, positivity/constraint residuals when part of the problem, integrator failures, retry or replay incidence and the reference error allowance. Track refusal by cause with overlaps, epoch, regime and spatial clustering, and break down solve wall time and work counts by refused vs all inputs. Keep distributions for diagnosis, with the maximum the criterion. [p17 loop](../panel/p17_audit.py#L59) · [Same-input check](#read-only-checks) · [Solver work counters](../../src/problems/ode/system.py#L91)

2. **Wall time and the fallback tail.** Measure synchronized split-step latency at p50, p95, p99 and maximum, cells per second under steady load, deadline misses, and startup and retry cost separately. Use paired exact-only and hybrid runs from identical initial states, interleaved, with device events for stream-local stages and wall time for the complete pipeline. Record live and fallback batch size distributions, occupancy, dispatches, scan/compact/scatter time, queue wait, transfer bytes and synchronization, and whether difficult states bunch in one batch or rank. Sweep immediate fallback, grouping by a cheap predicted workload, and CPU vs GPU fallback, including the small-subset overhead for each. Queue across independent tasks only within their allowed wait; a cell's next split stage cannot run on an unreturned fallback. Measure the proposed asynchronous overlap on an actual timeline. A low mean refusal can still produce a long split-step tail, so require 2× at the declared workload and check latency against its deadline. 3. **Memory, account movement and numerical decisions.** Report resident and peak allocated and reserved bytes, model bytes per device, temporary bytes per live cell, bytes moved per step and the largest batch before throttling or exhausting memory. The existing FP64 account is one persistent eight-byte value per cell with at least one logical read and write per update, besides temporary vectors. A verified componentwise enclosure can use `8o` bytes/cell and add halo traffic. Measure whether its narrower refusal pays for those costs versus a single conservative scalar; include cell IDs and transfer, resets and migration. Ensure exactly one account update for each committed step, including retry and reordering. Measure cold startup, warm resident execution, host-origin transfers, output casts and threshold flips by precision. Time FP64 reference/guard recomputations and count what they add to refusal or the tail.
