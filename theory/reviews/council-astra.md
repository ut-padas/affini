**Recommendation.** Repair the dense-KIDA reference comparison first. On 48 validation states I gave the deployed invariant projection the solver’s answer itself. Every state then missed 0.01 dex, with a maximum of 293 dex. The raw polynomial also fails, by much less. I would keep rollout-tuned ChebTT for small boxes, improve the chart’s sampling where its large-lattice rollouts already work, and give online local fitting one bounded trial under mixing. Spend GPU time only if the learned predictor first shows useful accurate coverage, and grant deployment credit only for fresh coupled chains at a measured net speed gain. ## 1. What section 9 gets right and what changes the decision

1. **The largest target error has a demonstrated cause downstream of learning.** On the same 48 fold-0 validation pairs, the selected KIDA chart size and degree gave a raw worst of 3.015 dex, unchanged by the output margin, and 294.23 dex after projection. Passing exact solver labels through projection gave 293.06 dex. The inferred matrix had 373 rows for 578 free species. The code infers laws by a relative SVD cutoff on normalized RHS samples, solves a relative-change least-squares problem per query, then floors negative abundances at `1e-300`. An ill-conditioned correction, spurious inferred laws, or both deserve testing. The experiment isolates the stage, without distinguishing those mechanisms. Test physical conservation rows derived from the actual network, scale the constraints, and check positivity, conservation and exact-label round trips together. Some grain processes or external reservoirs may require different balances. Use an unprojected answer as a diagnostic arm. [Check below](#reproducing-the-two-projection-checks) · [invariant inference](../../src/benchmark/step_maps.py) · [deployed projection](../panel/generic2.py) · [network wrapper](../../src/problems/ode/astro.py)

2. **Replace S4’s reconstruction veto with a test of the step.** For a damped direction, two states can differ greatly and have almost the same successor. Conversely, an accurate reconstruction can displace a sensitive species just enough to alter the next step. The original lift check compares successors when the guard permits a solve. Its infinity also covers an overlarge design, nonfinite reconstruction and a one-unit error on *any* free component. For example, at KIDA `k = 20` plus four held inputs a quartic has 20,475 columns versus fewer than 4,800 fit pairs. Infinity there says nothing about a fitted quartic. The all-infinite curve’s tie returns `k = 1`. It does not measure a necessary dimension. Nelson actually validated `k = 12` of 14 free variables with lift error 0.000164 dex, and primordial `k = 6` of 9 at 0.00101. Both refused every input. Neither failure by itself prioritizes a new decoder. The population lower bound in Proposition 6.5 requires its geometric hypotheses. A Jacobian rank or PCA count does not establish those hypotheses for KIDA. [Lift logic](../panel/generic.py) · [KIDA](../panel/data/records/r4b/kida_dense.json) · [Nelson](../panel/data/records/r4b/nelson.json) · [primordial](../panel/data/records/r4b/primordial.json) · [memo T6 and §8](../MEMO.md)

3. **Judge the coupled service, including the cost of refusals.** Among the 37 step maps, 26 pass held-out selection, 22 of those hold on the full ordinary test rollouts, 20 also hold on both mixed chains, and only **18** also solve less than half the steps in each mixed case. These are nested empirical counts, obtained by filtering the served rows on the unrounded errors and shares. R4b’s reported speed uses refusal on exact trajectory inputs and separate batched gate and prediction timings. POLLU’s 5.79× uses 10.2% refusal, versus 89.0% solved on its rollout, and 100% on both mixed chains. HIRES’s single-mix accepted maximum is 0.00220 at weight 0.01, while its chained deviation is 0.216 there. A fallback removes the new defect at that step and propagates earlier ones. Score both quantities. Time the complete executed path and compare with the solver on the same workload. If expensive states are preferentially refused, use their fraction of baseline *time* for the speed ceiling. 4. **Keep S1, change its controls.** Separate training, calibration, choice of configuration, and final audit by whole tracer/environment history. R4b calibrates on a validation fold’s one-step errors then uses that fold’s chains to select degree and ridge. Its 99% quantile pools states. Preserve the known test set as a regression set and reserve new histories for adjudication. Cross random versus leverage-selected starts at fixed degree and label budget, then cross mixed labels in training versus mixed labels in calibration. Charge the offset solve as well as the subsequent step. Pivoted QR for initial unisolvence, greedy leverage updates and maximizing the exact sum of absolute weights are distinct design choices. Give random coverage a quota. Early steps merit emphasis on Fisher–KPP, but at the selected degree and ridge the three worst-step indices are 39/39/39 on Brusselator d2, 38/18/11 on Van der Pol and 14/20/19 on KIDA. 5. **Retain a learned comparator.** The linear head gives a useful sensitivity to sample values. The approximation error at the query remains unknown even for that head. Proposition 5.1 needs reproduction of the space. POLLU’s selected design has rank 229 for 231 terms. Ridge introduces bias. A TT cross even with fixed pivot indices can involve a sampled pivot *value* in a denominator (rank-one matrix cross gives $A_{ij_0}A_{i_0j}/A_{i_0j_0}$). A local Taylor record with a Jacobian is not an ordinary value-only least-squares fit either. Require the actual reproduction identity before invoking T5. For branch switching, input-dependent projection and fallback, use the full-state recurrence or prove an augmented-encoder tube. The empirical comparison between a linear and nonlinear predictor is fair on this project’s observed-maximum metric. Neither has a verified error bound for the deployed KIDA chain. ## 5. Minimum evidence for a learned astrochemistry surrogate

**CPU screen.** Validate exact-label round trips and the reference solver on a stratified subset with tighter tolerances, especially at the scoring floor and for species crossing it. Freeze the floor, unit, species mask, environment range and step size. On grouped dense-KIDA data compare persistence, multi-output affine residual and one small full-state residual MLP, with identical input/target transforms, invariant treatment and calibration. Use a limited label-count curve (one quarter versus all available training tracers) and log the worst species at each stage. Fit the direct next step or increment, including all free species, with a loss that gives hard scored outputs weight. Report both masked errors and evolution of the unscored state. The decisive cheap number is the fraction of baseline solver time on states whose *raw deployed* one-step error exceeds the proposed local budget. Even a perfect rejector has to pay that amount if every accepted step must satisfy that budget. Compute it separately on clean and solver-labelled mixed inputs. A poor gate with good oracle coverage is a detector problem. Poor oracle coverage at both sample budgets is evidence against scaling that predictor. [Score mask](../../src/problems/ode/step_maps.py) · [KIDA data and tolerances](../recipe/r3_kida.py) · [factor accounting](../MEMO.md)

**Existing read-only screen, post hoc.** On the 160/40 training-tracer split used below, a full-input linear residual and the same fit with 256 frozen random tanh features each had **zero of 960** validation inputs with every scored species inside 0.01 dex, even before projection. Median/worst were 0.250/177.5 and 0.267/240.1 dex. These deliberately simple unregularized heads are weak controls, especially in sparse directions. This is evidence against spending the next run enlarging a random dictionary. It leaves supervised features, regularization, and a better placed training set open. Reproduction from the dense training chunk files: sort by chunk index, concatenate and take 200, permute with NumPy seed 100, reserve the first 40, flatten time pairs per tracer, floor input and target logs at −14 on free species, preserve held inputs, standardize each input using training mean and standard deviation floored at 0.1. Fit the clipped next-state increment by NumPy least squares with intercept and `rcond=1e-8`. For the second fit append `tanh(a @ W + b)` to standardized inputs `a`. Draw `W` and `b` from standard normals with independent seeds 19 and 20, and divide `W` by the square root of 582. Score against the original true next state above −12. ## 6. Order, budget and stopping decisions

These are proposed one-core **compute caps**, excluding implementation, anchored by the recorded KIDA selection time of 1,578 s, approximately 0.2 s per dense step, and the method-specific recorded builds. Log actual labels, wall time, peak memory, batch size and the number of queries required to amortize training. For growing methods include independent auditing in the deployment bill if deployment requires it. Use the repository’s per-case record store for long runs. 1. **Reference and scorer, first.** Allow 30 CPU minutes for the exact-label, near-exact and stricter-solver tests on Nelson and dense KIDA. Validate the physical constraint rows and the final floored output. Require the reference/postprocessing discrepancy to use at most 0.001 dex on the scored probe. Record stage-wise maxima before rerunning expensive selection. If this budget fails, resolve the reference contract before ranking models.
2. **Test the target directly.** Cap the affine/MLP and label-budget screen at two CPU hours per network problem, with a predeclared small width, finite epoch limit and seeds. Include 100–400 stratified mixed labels for calibration and use a disjoint set for evaluation. Run Nelson as a control and dense KIDA as the decision case. If only Nelson passes, pursue the diagnosed data or postprocessing issue once. A smaller network alone does not justify a GPU campaign.
3. **Run two inexpensive comparisons to guide existing schemes.** Cap at 30 CPU minutes per pilot for cached-start design on Fisher d3/Burgers d20, and TT versus chart under mixed chains on Fisher d3/CHEMAKZO. For HIRES compare fixed-chart mixing with an added normal coordinate and cold versus growing SlowTable. Give local reuse one additional hour on Nelson. Expand a winning arm to the other panel classes after it changes a common-pass decision. Stop growth if its record creation cost cannot be amortized, even if the last few queries hit.
4. **Freeze finalists and audit closed chains.** Budget two CPU hours initially for fresh dense-KIDA cell groups with both mixing strengths. At the recorded average solve time a 40-cell, 24-step reference costs roughly three minutes per history group before burn-in, fallback and overhead. Extend the audit only for qualifying arms. If learned and classical models both pass, choose the lower total wall time at the intended query volume. If the learned arm has no advantage, retain the existing scheme.
5. **GPU and MHD only for a survivor.** Budget one CPU hour to package frozen weights, reference cases and streaming timing inputs. Measure repeated batched GPU runs and, when available, an actual coupled patch with variable step size. Use its measured integration cost to set the patch budget. A failure there limits the validated regime to the successful proxy. Keep the solver for the rest.

**Stop now:** the all-panel rerun to “finish” completed R4b, diffuse KIDA outside the supported rate range, unconditional AFINNI/ART sweeps, full-state finite-difference tables at 582 inputs, and architecture surveys (PINNs, neural RHS, transformers or foundation models). Defer learned routing, a descriptor-based winner classifier and rational-axis implementation until the candidate methods have comparable coupled outcomes. Beating “always ART” on censored records would not establish a better choice than rollout-tuned TT, the chart or the solver. ### Reproducing the two projection checks

Read-only, post hoc, on the dense **training** release at commit `c758db4`. From `theory/panel` run the following with `SURROGATE_DEVICE=cpu SURROGATE_MPI=off PYTHONPATH=../..:.:../recipe OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 ../../.venv/bin/python -B`. Both probes used 48 of the 960 fold-0 validation pairs. The held environment in each trial answer is the input environment, as in deployment. The truth mask excludes the held columns. There are 111–194 scored free species per sampled state. These checks use cached labels and no new chemistry steps. [Projection](../panel/generic2.py) · [data loader](../panel/r4_generic.py)

```python
import numpy as np
from r4_generic import kida
from generic import split, pairs
from generic2 import build

P, train, _ = kida("dense")
fit, val = split(train, 0)
s, _, _ = build(P, fit, 1, 3, 0.0, 1)
X, Y = pairs(val)
idx = np.random.default_rng(713).choice(len(X), 48, replace=False)
X, Y = X[idx], Y[idx]
m = P.scored(Y)
f = s.T.free
print(s.T.W.shape)
for amplitude in (0.0, 0.001):
    raw = Y.copy()
    raw[:, f:] = X[:, f:]
    raw[:, :f] += amplitude * np.random.default_rng(24).uniform(-1, 1, raw[:, :f].shape)
    err = np.where(m, abs(s.project(X, raw) - Y), 0.0)
    print(amplitude, abs(raw - Y)[m].max(), err.max(), (err.max(axis=1) > 0.01).sum())

raw = X.copy()
raw[:, :f] = s.G(s.T.chart(X, s.k))
clipped = raw.copy()
clipped[:, :f] = np.clip(raw[:, :f], s.T.zlo[:f], s.T.zhi[:f])
for stage in (X, raw, clipped, s.project(X, clipped)):
    e = np.where(m, abs(stage - Y), 0.0).max(axis=1)
    print(np.median(e), e.max())
```
