## Findings

### F1 — Critical — The rollout audit answers a different accuracy question

**Location.** Proposition 4.1, Corollary 4.1′, P7.2, §9 U7 and §10 item 1.

**Problem and evidence.** The audited score can be the maximum over times and output components for one initial state. The probability bound then concerns the mass of initial states where that score exceeds tolerance. An arbitrarily high error on a set of mass below $\eta$ meets that guarantee. Also, “with probability $1-\alpha$” is a repeated-sampling guarantee on ever accepting a bad model. The conditional probability of accepting a bad model given that there was an acceptance can be one. For example, audit a single fixed bad model whose violation mass is strictly between $\eta$ and one. If the model passes by chance, the accepted model is bad.

**Fix.** Before each audit, freeze the candidate, the score, the initial-state law and the sample count conditional on the past. For round $r$, the probability of a false pass conditional on the past is at most $(1-\eta)^{N_r}$. Union bound over attempted rounds starting at $r=1$ and state the result as an upper bound on the probability of ever accepting a candidate with violation mass greater than $\eta$. Count exceptions, nonphysical states and undefined steps as failures unless fallback is part of the frozen deployed map. With numerical reference trajectories, use a validated reference error margin if the target is the exact flow. For worst-case accuracy, separately require a uniform certificate, or a proved modulus of continuity of the rollout score and a lower bound on the sampling mass of every small ball in the target set. For example, if every ball of radius $h$ centred in the target set has mass strictly above $\eta$ and the score is $L$-Lipschitz there, mass at most $\eta$ above $t$ implies a uniform bound $t+Lh$. Keep the audit as a complementary distributional guarantee.

### F2 — Critical — Theorem 8's residual is unavailable from the fitted model and the sampled check does not verify the bound

**Location.** Theorem 8, lines 419–436 and 459–463, §9 U2 and §10 item 1.

**Problem and evidence.** $\varepsilon_G$ is the supremum of the defect against the exact map over all of $M$. The 1.18e-3 in `t2_slow_surrogate.json` is a maximum on finitely many training and test states. The constants $36$ and $0.997$ come from central differences on 4,001 points in `t2_slow_surrogate.py`. Neither procedure gives a validated upper bound. The finite list of sampled states has no demonstrated forward invariance. The computed 1.53 dex and observed 5.2e-4 dex agree with the reported arithmetic but cannot validate a uniform 39-step theorem or quantify its pessimism. The rollout also clips the output. A verification must either use the clipped model in all constants or establish that clipping never activates on the certified domain.

**Fix.** State the result for $n\ge1$ with $U_\rho=\{u:d(u,U)\le\rho\}$, or use strict radius inequalities for an open tube. Induct only through the required inputs $u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1}$. The proof uses no injectivity. Keep injectivity as a separate choice for representing $M$. For a finite-horizon study replace invariance by coverage of all exact input states through step $n-1$ and use the appropriate common domain or time-dependent domains. Bound the actual fitted map's Lipschitz constants using polynomial derivatives, interval bounds or a verified piecewise enclosure. A pointwise Jacobian bound on a nonconvex tube alone need not give the asserted global Lipschitz constant. A verified $h$-net of $M$, a defect Lipschitz bound $L_\delta$ and oracle error bound $b$ would give $\varepsilon_G\le\max_i\|\Psi(x_i)-\widetilde\Phi(x_i)\|+L_\delta h+b$. Call 1.53 a diagnostic estimate until these checks are done. Global Lipschitz constants of the model can be large even with an exact fit on $M$. A tighter candidate recurrence uses the separately bounded latent defect $\sup_M\|\hat g(\hat\pi x)-\hat\pi\Phi(x)\|$ in place of $L_\pi\varepsilon_G$. A first-order alternative needs a remainder and a bound on where its linearisation is valid.

### F3 — Critical — Assumption (S) excludes the positive-codimension case it is meant to describe

**Location.** Lines 315–329, Theorem 6 and Corollary 6.1, Proposition 8.2 and §10 items 2–3.

**Problem and evidence.** As written, the derivative remainder is bounded by a factor proportional to $\operatorname{dist}(p,S)$. At $t=0$ and $p\in S$, this forces $I-D\pi(p)=0$. Yet $\pi$ maps into $S$, so $\operatorname{rank}D\pi(p)\le k<d$. For positive time the same contradiction follows from invertibility of $D\Phi_t(p)$ for a smooth flow and $\pi(p)=p$. This is a contradiction in the hypothesis on an ambient neighbourhood, independent of whether the test problems admit Fenichel reduction.

**Fix.** Require the distance factor only for the state remainder. State the separate hypothesis $\sup_N\|D(\Phi_t-\Phi_t\circ\pi)\|\le C_1e^{-\gamma_1t}$ if the singular-value implication is needed. Then use the rank of $D(\Phi_t\circ\pi)$ and the singular-value perturbation inequality. Make that derivative estimate an explicit extra assumption until an applicable derivative tracking theorem or proof is given. A smooth vector field alone supplies neither the relevant foliation regularity nor a verified neighbourhood covering an application box. State the precise invariance and domain of $S$, $N$ and the phase projection. Proposition 8.2 is an algebraically valid one-step split with this repaired state hypothesis, a single chart on the image of $\pi$ (or a specified atlas), and uniform errors on the displayed domains. Its proof does not use convexity if $\hat G$ is already Lipschitz there. In particular it needs a fit on $\chi(\pi(N))$, which data on $M\subset S$ do not give. Fibre constancy by itself is insufficient unless the encoded base point is also correct in the chosen coordinates. [Eldering, Kvalheim and Revzen, global stable foliations and their regularity](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03646).

### F4 — Critical — The singular-value staircase does not upper-bound the number of global surrogate coordinates

**Location.** T6 opening, Corollaries 6.1–6.4, lines 347–349, P6.1, §9 U3 and §10 item 2.

**Problem and evidence.** Theorem 6.6 and Proposition 6.7 bound singular values at a point or along one path. They do not construct an encoder and cannot bound the global count under the stated geometric hypotheses. Even a smooth stable-fibre projection onto a compact one-dimensional slow manifold can require two real coordinates in a continuous global encoder. The exact counterexample below has uniformly small second singular value on an annulus and an order-one obstruction to one real coordinate. The same example refutes $m_M\le\dim M$. In the other direction, a coarse tolerance can admit a constant map even on a high-dimensional domain, so $\dim M\le m_{\mathrm{ball}}$ is also false in general. The two minima are ordered by restriction when the target sets are nested and the same encoders, norm and tolerance are used.

**Fix.** Define the counts at a stated tolerance, horizon if relevant, target set and encoder class. Separate local differential rank, the lower bound from Proposition 6.5, and a constructive upper bound from a verified global embedding or chart atlas. Under corrected (S), an embedding $\chi:S\to\mathbb R^q$ gives the factorisation through $q$ real coordinates with the state remainder of Theorem 6. Taking $q=k$ needs an additional chart hypothesis. For an atlas, specify patch selection and overlap behaviour. Report Robertson's two large ambient singular values and one successful fitted coordinate on one sampled leaf as observations. HIRES's local PCA median is evidence about the sampled geometry at that resolution. With finitely many starts the exact finite trajectory data cannot establish a three-dimensional manifold.

### F5 — Major — Proposition 2.4(b) omits a hypothesis and P2.1 does not follow from normal stability

**Location.** Proposition 2.4(a)–(c), P2.1–P2.2, §9 U2 and §10 item 1.

**Problem and evidence.** Case (b) needs the exact-map contraction $d(\Phi(p),S)\le\kappa d(p,S)$ from (a), explicitly stated. Otherwise take $S=\{0\}$, $\Phi(p)=2p$ and $\Psi=\Phi$. The two written defect conditions hold with zero defect. With the contraction assumption included, (a) and (b) are triangle inequalities and (c) is immediate. Specify $0\le\kappa_\Psi<1$ and $\varepsilon_S\ge0$. Normal stability says nothing about the accumulation of tangential phase errors. For example, take the attracting circle below, and a surrogate that radially projects and rotates by the exact angle plus a small bias. It satisfies case (c) with zero normal offset. For inputs on the circle the surviving error equals the original defect in Euclidean norm, while the phase error adds the bias each step. A uniform one-step error still predicts rollout via Proposition 2.1 when its Lipschitz and coverage hypotheses hold, regardless of normal stability.

**Fix.** Remove the implication in P2.1 and the converse “without it, neither does.” Show survival as a diagnostic and use tangent/normal defect bounds plus propagation in each component for a theorem. Ratios of sampled maxima do not establish sign coherence or comparative predictive accuracy. In particular, a ratio near a first-order norm envelope does not establish componentwise signs along an orbit. Report P2.2 as a hypothesis until the weighted defect vectors or their autocorrelation have been measured. For a fixed conservation leaf, formulate the tube relative to that leaf. A full ambient tube includes distinct invariant values, so exact conservation does not contract those values towards a single leaf. In log coordinates impose $C\,10^{\Psi(z)}=C\,10^z$ where $C$ acts on physical concentrations. Feeding an invariant to the fit does not impose that identity.

### F6 — Major — The Robertson test does not establish sharpness of Proposition 6.5

**Location.** Proposition 6.5, lines 332–345, §10 item 2 and the proof-status list.

**Problem and evidence.** The ball argument, including the factor $1/2$ on $K_2r^2$, is correct. Call the Robertson check a scale comparison. [DeVore, Howard and Micchelli, lower-bound argument via antipodal points](https://gwern.net/doc/cs/algorithm/1989-devore.pdf).

Pointwise positive $r_j(0;x_0)$ need not have a positive infimum without a uniformity hypothesis. For a fixed tube, the running maximum $\Lambda$ is nondecreasing in the horizon. A largest product ellipse need not exist as a single coordinatewise maximum. Give the degree claim as a sufficient bound with $M$ retained and state $c,p>0$ for Corollary 1.4's regime formulas. The converse in §1 is a root rate $\limsup_n E_n^{1/n}=1/\rho_*$ in one variable, without a guaranteed two-sided prefactor. **Numerical scope.** The Robertson and Brusselator scripts sample starting points and times, and the former samples complex boundary phases. The Brusselator uses entrywise bounds around sampled real trajectories, in physical coordinates on the exponentiated box. The reported 1,272–32,236 are $\log(100)/\log\rho$ proxies, which omit the prefactors of the interpolation bound. ### F8 — Major — The empirical least-squares variance does not follow by multiplying the exact-Gram result by a norm

**Location.** Propositions 5.1–5.2, lines 286–301, §9 U5.

**Problem and evidence.** The Lebesgue inequality and pointwise identity are correct for unweighted least squares with full column rank. The displayed variance is correct for the estimator that replaces the empirical Gram matrix by the population identity, assuming i.i.d. sampling and an orthonormal basis. The random $G^{-1}$ depends on the same points as the residual vector. Its multiplication can create bias and change the directions of fluctuations. Even multiplying a scalar variance by $\|G^{-1}\|^2$ does not prove the assertion. For example, take one normalised feature whose values at two equiprobable points are $(1,\sqrt3)/\sqrt2$, and residual values $(\sqrt3,-1)$, orthogonal to that feature. With $N=1$ the empirical coefficient correction is $r(X)/\varphi(X)$ and has nonzero mean, although $\varphi(X)r(X)$ has mean zero.

**Fix.** Define $G=N^{-1}V^TV$ with $V_{ij}=\varphi_j(x_i)$, $K(x)=\|\varphi(x)\|_2^2$, and $K=\sup K(x)$. State the deterministic bound on the correction as $\sqrt{K(x)}\|G^{-1}\|\|b\|$, where $b=N^{-1}\sum_i\varphi(X_i)r(X_i)$. For independent draws, $\mathbb E\|b\|^2=N^{-1}\mathbb E[K(X)r(X)^2]$. Combine a bound on $\|b\|$ with a separate conditioning event if an empirical high-probability result is wanted. The population standard-deviation scale is an absolute upper bound in terms of $\|r\|_\infty$, not a relative estimate in terms of $|r(x)|$. ART's sampling and adaptive conditioning need their own hypotheses. The three-of-four result is from the synthetic scratch functions. It suggests a mechanism for U5 but does not identify the cause of the specific ART benchmark failure.

### F10 — Major — The proved staircase inequalities are valid, but the path-average extrapolation in (c) is too strong

**Location.** Theorem 6.6, Proposition 6.7(a)–(c), the checks and P6.1.

**Problem and evidence.** Theorem 6.6's exterior-power/log-norm argument works for fixed invertible $W$ in the Euclidean norm. The constant-matrix inequality in 6.7(a) is correct, including the infimum over eigenbases. The POLLU bracket shifts the threshold by roughly 2–4 decades **in each direction**. The end-to-end span is about 4–7.7 decades. The endpoint norms, Ky Fan bound and product estimate in 6.7(b) are correct. A moving frame is an extension of 6.6, with a connection term and endpoint factors absent from a fixed weight. Take the $m$th root of the product bound to upper-bound $\sigma_m$. If that root is at most the threshold, the count strictly above the threshold is at most $m-1$. The estimate gives no lower bound on those singular values. A count agreement between frozen and measured spectra on a nonlinear path is an observation, since (a) assumes a constant matrix.

A small basis rotation relative to a spectral gap is insufficient for the uniform-in-horizon comparison in (c). Let $J(t)=R(\omega t)\operatorname{diag}(0,-b)R(\omega t)^T$ where $R$ is planar rotation and $0<|\omega|\ll b$. The instantaneous slow exponent is zero. In the rotating frame the exponents are $(-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4\omega^2})/2$, both strictly negative. The leading singular value thus decays exponentially with time, even with an arbitrarily small ratio $|\omega|/b$. This can be realised along an autonomous trajectory by adjoining $s'=1$ and using $z'=J(s)z$. The extra clock contributes a separate neutral direction. A roughness theorem can preserve separated rates while changing their values.

**Fix.** Retain (a) and (b) with their matrix assumptions and norms. Call them diagnostics for local differential rank, with lower dimension bounds coming from a lower enclosure of $s$ and an upper enclosure of $K_2$ as in 6.5. For (c), formulate a finite-time estimate with explicit accumulated perturbations, or assume hypotheses that control cumulative rate shifts. If constants may depend arbitrarily on the horizon, the stated comparison has no rate content. In the Neumann example, state the grid convention and $W=I$. At fixed positive $D,\tau$ and bounded $L_f$, the estimate gives a mesh-independent singular-value count at a fixed threshold. It does not imply a grid-independent global surrogate dimension or cost.

### F11 — Major — Corollaries 6.2 and 6.3 omit essential approximation terms or hypotheses

**Location.** Lines 324–330 and §8/§10 uses of the fibre approximation.

**Problem and evidence.** Half the oscillation is the pointwise fibre minimax error for a scalar output, and for the max norm with independently chosen component midranges. For a vector Euclidean error it is a lower bound on the radius of a smallest enclosing ball, with strict inequality for an equilateral triangle. The identity also allows arbitrary decoders. Continuity and extension of the decoder are additional issues. In slow-fast standard form, an $O(\varepsilon)$ upper estimate needs a uniform fast attraction estimate and control of the shift in the slow variable across the relevant fibres. There is no universal order-$\varepsilon$ lower bound. A decoupled slow equation has zero fibre tilt. Corollary 6.3 allocates the full tolerance to fitting $\Gamma_\tau$ and $\pi$ while dropping $\|R_\tau\|$.

**Fix.** Set the output norm and decoder class in 6.2. For (6.3), require that the approximation to $\pi$ land in $S$ or that $\Gamma_\tau$ and its approximation be defined on a suitable common extension. Use
```text
total error ≤ decoder error + Lip(Γτ) × projection error + sup_N ||Rτ||.
```
Assign three budgets. State an $O(\varepsilon)$ fibre-tilt bound only under sufficient slow-fast hypotheses for the specified domain. Treat the 0.999 measured slope as a check of one example. Corollary 6.4 is the componentwise form of Theorem 6. Transferring a Chebyshev rate to the reduced decoder would require regularity in its chart and an appropriate approximation domain.

## Own test

Consider the exact polynomial ODE on the plane
```text
x' = ω J x + a(1 - ||x||₂²)x,    J = [[0, -1], [1, 0]],    a ≫ |ω| > 0.
```
In polar coordinates the phase rotates at rate $\omega$ and the radius solves $r'=a(1-r^2)r$. On the compact invariant annulus $1/2\le r\le3/2$, the radius at time $t$ is
```text
r_t = [1 + (r⁻² - 1) exp(-2at)]⁻¹/².
```
The unit circle $S$ is normally attracting with smooth phase projection $\pi(x)=x/\|x\|_2$. Differentiate the radial solution: its radial derivative is uniformly $O(e^{-2at})$ on the annulus. The other singular direction has gain $r_t/r$. On the circle the singular values are exactly $1$ and $e^{-2at}$. In particular the second singular value is uniformly small on the annulus at a fixed positive step with large enough $a$. The state tracking estimate of corrected (S) holds by the same radial derivative bound. The derivative remainder has a nonzero radial derivative on the circle at finite time, as expected after correcting (S).

For any continuous scalar encoder on the annulus, Borsuk–Ulam forces equal codes at an antipodal pair on $S$. The exact step rotates the pair and preserves its Euclidean separation of 2. At least one decoded output therefore has error at least 1, on $S$ alone. The max-norm obstruction is at least $1/\sqrt{2}$. Two real coordinates suffice by the identity encoder. This rules out a global one-real-coordinate interpretation of “rank one” even for a smooth stiff flow with explicit fibres. The counterexample also applies when the target is the visited periodic orbit.

## Assessment of the foundation

Item 1 is a sound finite-horizon starting point for a specified initial-state set and autonomous surrogate map. The important feature is that propagation can be bounded where the learned latent dynamics actually run. The theorem by itself gives no verified model, uniform accuracy claim, convergence rate under refinement or guarantee for repeated operator splitting. The Robertson and HIRES results justify further testing of the design. They leave open whether the certificates can be made both rigorous and small enough to meet a useful tolerance at competitive cost.

A first demonstration could use a compact domain on a fixed conservation leaf with a verified chart and a cheap independent reference enclosure. Specify the initial set and the horizon, construct a state reconstruction that enforces the invariants and positivity, measure or bound the latent defect independently of the physical output defect, and enclose the model's return into its validated domain. A deterministic net with a proven defect modulus gives a simple end-to-end baseline. For fixed horizon, if the verified residual tends to zero while the map gains and tube radii stay uniformly controlled, Theorem 8 gives uniform convergence. A rate then requires a rate for the residual as a function of fill distance, polynomial degree or oracle work. A learned chart needs verified regularity and coverage of the relevant chart image for that step. Multi-step Lipschitz bounds can replace powers of a global one-step gain when useful, provided the required iterates remain in the verified domain.

Item 2 can inform chart selection, anisotropic sampling and tests for an unresolved direction. Proposition 6.5 supplies a rigorous obstruction under validated local constants. The spectral tests supply candidate dimensions. A constructive upper result requires additional geometry and approximation estimates. For example, verify a global projection along fibres on the chosen domain and an embedding or atlas of its image, then bound the physical error terms in Proposition 8.2. This is a viable theorem for a stated class. For the split application, define the actual inputs after the other operators, including conservation changes. Certify the composite map or verify the chemistry domain and gains for every kicked state. If the domain is too large, quantify coverage and include a validated solver fallback in the map being certified. A pure-chemistry trajectory audit cannot supply that deployment guarantee.
