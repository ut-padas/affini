# Prior art for the theory memo (T1–T7, section 8 and section G)

This file covers the prior-art search behind the seven candidate theorems of the theory memo. For each theorem it
lists the closest published work, what that work proves, and whether the theorem is already known. It follows
[docs/LITERATURE.md](../docs/LITERATURE.md): works already in that register are cited by their IDs (VII-1, II-24,
...) and not reviewed again. New works get IDs TH-1, TH-2, ..., unique across this file. Search date: 2026-11-21.

**Verified.** *yes*: I read the source itself (full text, or the theorem statements quoted). *abstract-only*: I
read the abstract on arXiv or the publisher page, or a bibliographic summary of it. *unverified*: cited from
memory, or seen only as a reference or search snippet. Constants and theorem numbers are given only when they
were read in the source. Anything else is marked.

## T1. Analyticity radii of a flow map in the initial state that do not shrink with stiffness

| ID | Reference | Verified | What it proves (precise) | Relevance / gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TH-1 | Hansen, Schwab 2013, *Sparse adaptive approximation of high dimensional parametric initial value problems*, Vietnam J. Math. 41:181–215 (pages from memory), doi:10.1007/s10013-013-0011-9. Read as ETH SAM Report 2011-64, https://www.sam.math.ethz.ch/sam_reports/reports_final/reports2011/2011-64.pdf | yes (report version) | ODE X' = f(t,X;y) on a Banach space S. The parameters enter **affinely**, f = f0 + Σ y_j f_j with y ∈ [−1,1]^N, and each f_j is Lipschitz on balls B_R with constant L_j(R) and f_j(t,0)=0. Thm 1 needs a **small-data condition** sup‖x0‖ ≤ (1−κ) R e^{−L(R)T/κ}. Under it the solution exists, and its dependence on the data is Lipschitz with factor e^{L(R)T/κ}/(1−κ) (eqs. 18–19). Thms 4–5: if the f_j are also analytic in x, X(t;·) extends holomorphically to a polydisc whose polyradius ρ_j is set by L_j(R) and a free δ (the formula is garbled in my text extraction). Under ℓ^p summability of (L_j) the Taylor coefficients are ℓ^p-summable, so best N-term truncation converges at rate N^{−(1/p−1)} uniformly in y. | The closest result. Its constants come from the **two-sided Lipschitz constant** through e^{LT}, so the radii shrink with T and with stiffness (L ≥ the fast rate). Holomorphy is in the **parameters**: the initial state enters only as data x0(z) that is holomorphic in z. No logarithmic norm and no dissipativity are used. T1 instead replaces L by the log norm μ of the complexified Jacobian. |

Gives no a-priori radius or Chebyshev-decay theorem, and its remainders grow with the two-sided Lipschitz behaviour. |
| TH-11 | Cyranka 2014, *Efficient and generic algorithm for rigorous integration forward in time of dPDEs: Part I*, J. Sci. I recall that logarithmic norms control the dissipative tail in the dPDE work, but did not confirm it. | Possible prior use of log norms to get enclosures that do not deteriorate with dissipation. Real variables only, as far as I know. |
| TH-12 | Reagan, Najm, Ghanem, Knio 2003, *Uncertainty quantification in reacting-flow simulations through non-intrusive spectral projection*, Combust. Flame 132(3):545–555 | unverified (bibliographic) | Non-intrusive projection onto polynomial chaos (PC) for chemistry with uncertain rate parameters. | Polynomial surrogates of chemistry in **parameters**, empirical, with no analyticity radii. |
| TH-13 | Le Maître, Najm, Pébay, Ghanem, Knio 2007, *Multi-resolution-analysis scheme for uncertainty quantification in chemical systems*, SISC 29(2):864–889 | abstract-only (via search summary) | Multiwavelet PC with adaptivity in parameter space and time, for a chemical system with 8 random parameters. | TH-14 | Wan, Karniadakis 2006, *Long-term behavior of polynomial chaos in stochastic flow simulations*, CMAME 195:5582–5596; Gerritsma, van der Steen, Vos, Karniadakis 2010, *Time-dependent generalized polynomial chaos*, JCP 229:8333–8363 | abstract-only | gPC in random parameters loses accuracy over long times (random frequencies). Remedies: multi-element gPC, or time-dependent bases. | Empirical counterpart of T1's "radius shrinks like exp(−Λ)": for oscillators with random frequency, phase drift makes the map in the parameter less smooth over time. Neither paper gives a log-norm explanation. |

**Verdict for T1.** Holomorphic dependence on initial data is classical: Picard iteration on a complex neighbourhood gives it,
and Cauchy majorants bound the radius. The log-norm Gronwall bound for real perturbations is also classical (VII-1, TH-3).
For parametric ODEs, TH-1 is the closest analyticity-plus-approximation result. Its radii come from two-sided
Lipschitz constants through e^{L T/κ}, under a small-data condition, and holomorphy is in affine parameters, not in the
initial state. I found no work that applies the logarithmic norm to the **complexified** Jacobian on a tube around the
real trajectories to get Bernstein-polyellipse radii **independent of τ** (or of stiffness) for dissipative systems,
and radii shrinking like exp(−max ∫m) for expanding ones, nor the a-priori Chebyshev coefficient bounds that follow
for step maps. T1 therefore **appears new as a combination, as far as this search found**. The gap is
"log norm instead of Lipschitz constant, initial state instead of parameters, and a step map with a τ-uniform
radius". The ingredients are standard, so the memo should credit them. It should also carry over TH-1's
bootstrap: a small-data-type condition that keeps the complex trajectory inside the tube where the log-norm bound holds.

## T2. Rollout error of a surrogate step map, bias at a stable fixed point, n·ε against √n·ε growth

|---|---|---|---|---|
| TH-15 | Qin, Wu, Xiu 2019, *Data driven governing equations approximation using deep neural networks*, JCP 395:620–635, https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.05537 | yes (Sec. 4) | Lemma 4.1: if f is L-Lipschitz on D, the flow is Lipschitz with factor e^{Lt}. Thm 4.3, for the ResNet model y ↦ y + N(y): E^{(m)} ≤ (1+e^{LΔ})^m E^{(0)} + ‖N − Φ_Δ‖_{L∞} ((1+e^{LΔ})^m − 1)/e^{LΔ}. | The standard learned-flow-map bound. | TH-19 | Stuart, Humphries 1996, *Dynamical Systems and Numerical Analysis*, Cambridge Univ. | TH-21 | Flow-map learning for NVAR models, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31438 (authors not checked) | unverified (snippet) | Error recurrence e_{n+1} ≤ L e_n + ε_train with an effective Lipschitz constant. One-step training error and rollout accuracy can diverge (prediction-error versus simulation-error fitting). | Shows the recurrence is standard in current ML work; none of these papers allocates the tolerance by mode. |

**Verdict for T2.** The first part, the rollout bound e_n ≤ ε Σ L^k with L from a log norm, is **known**. It is the
global-error argument for one-step methods (Lady Windermere's fan), and in stiff form it is B-convergence or the
one-sided-Lipschitz analysis (TH-20, VII-10). Learned-surrogate papers repeat it with the cruder two-sided Lipschitz
constant (TH-15, TH-21). The fixed-point statement, bias = (I − DΦ(x*))^{−1} δ(x*), is the classical persistence of
hyperbolic fixed points under perturbation (TH-18, TH-19), so it is **known**. What I did not find stated for
surrogates is the consequence that only defect components along slow eigen-directions accumulate, amplified by about
1/(|λ_slow| τ), so the acceptance tolerance should be weighted by mode. The n·ε against √n·ε dichotomy from the
sign coherence of the defect is the old systematic-versus-random error-accumulation argument (as in statistical
rounding-error analysis; I did not verify a citation). Net: **known, in a different setting**. The new part is
small: a mode-weighted acceptance criterion for surrogates, which follows from these facts.

| TH-32 | Goeßmann, Götte, Roth, Sweke, Kutyniok, Eisert 2020, *Tensor network approaches for learning non-linear dynamical laws*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12388 | abstract-only | TT parametrizations of the **vector field** that encode locality, with analytic motivation and rank-adaptive learning. | Locality ⇒ low TT rank for the right-hand side, not for the time-τ flow map. **Verdict for T3.** Light-cone bounds for classical lattice ODEs are **known** (TH-22 to TH-26), as is the decay
of exp(tA) for sparse A with graph distance (TH-28). The bound T3 states, for bounded local Jacobians on a box,
follows from the standard Dyson-series/Gronwall argument. The step "light cone ⇒ tensor-network rank independent of
system size" is **known for quantum propagators** (TH-29) and, recently, for states of dissipative **linear**
nearest-neighbour ODEs (TH-30). I found no result that turns a classical light cone into (i) a statement that each
output of a **nonlinear flow map** depends on a window of initial states up to a superexponentially small error,
used for surrogate construction, or (ii) TT-rank bounds for x0 ↦ Φ_t(x0) that do not grow with d. T3 therefore
**appears new in this setting, as far as this search found**. One caveat for the memo: the rank bound for a function
of a window of w variables is independent of d but can be exponential in w (up to n^{⌊w/2⌋} on an n-point grid). The
d-independence is real, but it is useful only while the window ~ 2ℓΔt + 1 stays small, which is the light-cone time
scale of TH-27.

**Verdict for T4.** Both parts are **known**. (a) Adaptive reuse of a holdout breaks the validity of a fixed-sample
test (TH-38). A union bound over rounds with fresh points, or an alpha-spending schedule, is standard multiple-testing
practice, so a correct audit protocol is an application, not a theorem. (b) "Measure of violation η plus Lipschitz
constant L ⇒ sup error ≤ tol + c L η^{1/d}" is elementary: a violation set of measure η contains no ball larger than
about η^{1/d}. It appears in current form in TH-17 and in fill-distance arguments generally. The Hickernell line
(TH-33 to TH-35) is the useful prior art: guaranteed adaptive sup-norm approximation is proven only under an explicit
**cone** assumption, and only in 1-D (TH-34). Multivariate cone results bound coefficient-norm errors (TH-35). The
memo's honest framing: the curse in (b) shows that a sampled audit cannot certify a sup norm in high d without a

## Summary of novelty (most to least new)

1. **T3 (light cone ⇒ windowed dependence and TT ranks independent of d for nonlinear flow maps).** Light cones for
   classical lattices and "light cone ⇒ low MPS rank" for quantum propagators are known. Applying them to a functional
   TT of x0 ↦ Φ_t(x0) appears new. Caveat: the bound is independent of d but exponential in the window width.
2. **T1 (complexified log-norm ⇒ a priori radii of analyticity in the initial state).** New as a combination. The known
   version (TH-1) uses Lipschitz constants, parameters and e^{LT}. The memo's theorem is valid wherever tried but its radii
   do shrink with τ and stiffness in the cases computed, and it is informative only on small boxes; the claim of radii
   that do not shrink was not borne out.
3. **T6 (coordinate counts of stiff step maps).** The dynamics is classical (Fenichel, CSP, isochrons). What the memo
   proves is narrower than "stiffness ⇒ k effective inputs": a factorisation under the Fenichel hypothesis (S), a
   Borsuk–Ulam lower bound on the coordinate count (a specialisation of DeVore–Howard–Micchelli), compound-matrix and
   staircase bounds on singular values, and an example where local rank 1 coexists with coordinate count 2. The
   dimension-reduction literature gives L² bounds.
4. **T7 (approximability of chemistry step maps).** Not a theorem but a confirmed empirical gap: no TT or Chebyshev
   step map of a chemistry network and no TT theory for flow maps were found.
5. **T2 (rollout error, fixed-point bias).** Known: global error of one-step methods, B-convergence, persistence of
   hyperbolic fixed points. The new part is the surrogate reading: tolerance weighted by mode along slow directions.
6. **T5 (least squares for a quadratic leaf).** Known (CDL, Cohen–Migliorati). It is useful as a diagnostic, since
   ART's 20p is far below the sufficient sample sizes. No sup-norm guarantee exists for adaptive oblique trees.
7. **T4 (certificates).** Known: adaptive data analysis, alpha spending, fill-distance and Lipschitz arguments. The
   Hickernell cone framework is the right formal model, with a sup-norm guarantee proven only in 1-D.
8. **Section 8 (reduced surrogates, Theorem 8).** A specialisation of discrete Lipschitz error estimates for manifold
   Galerkin/LSPG and latent dynamics (Lee–Carlberg 2020; Farenga et al. 2024). The prospective contribution is a
   *verified* end-to-end certificate for a stated class of stiff step maps, not the estimate itself.
9. **Section G (values and Jacobians).** Classical: Hermite interpolation (TH-63) and derivative-augmented polynomial
   regression (TH-64). The first-order correction about a tabulated state with the step's Jacobian, an accuracy region
   and fallback to integration is ISAT's (TH-62). The memo's decoder-centred correction with a fitted Jacobian field
   $B(u)$ and consistent targets is a specific design within that idea, and is described at that level.

### Summary for `theory/APPROACHES.md`

1. **No source reports a controlled maximum error for a learned chemistry surrogate.** Reported metrics are means, MSE
   and, in NN-39, a 99th percentile (0.38–4.8 dex), chosen instead of the maximum on purpose.
2. **The off-data failure is basis-independent.** Combustion (NN-28, NN-31), atmospheric chemistry (NN-33, NN-34),
   climate (NN-72, NN-73) and astrochemistry (NN-38, NN-39) all find coupled drift or instability, fixed by data
   coverage or online training; the memo's U8 and R5 say the same for polynomial charts.
3. **The computable sampling factor survives only for models linear in their data once their nonlinear part is
   frozen** (random features, last-layer heads, kernels, fixed-pole rationals, mapped Chebyshev); for network classes
   uniform accuracy from samples is intractable (NN-65).
4. **Propagation, refusal accounting, audits and coordinate counts carry over to every family**; Lipschitz constants
   and range enclosures of networks are computable (NN-74 to NN-76).
5. **Approximation:** on analytic maps networks inherit polynomial rates (NN-60, NN-61); on fronts and interfaces
   their advantage is real (NN-63, and a single tanh unit is a planar front), which is the one panel failure (the
   Fisher–KPP and Allen–Cahn ladders) where a learned basis may help.
