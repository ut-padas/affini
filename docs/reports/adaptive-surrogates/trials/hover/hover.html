1 A trial: symbols that explain themselves

How to try it. In the HTML version, rest the pointer on a symbol in an equation, or tab to it: a card shows what it is, its formula, its name in the code and the section that defines it, and every other occurrence of the symbol lights up. In the PDF, click a symbol to jump to its entry in the glossary at the end; the versions with tooltips also show a short definition on hover, in viewers that support it.

2 The fit

A cluster’s centre is c, the mean of its points, and its model is 𝑓̂(𝑥)=𝑤⊤(𝑥−c)+𝑏.

3 Where a leaf can be trusted

The scatter matrix Σ=∑𝑖(𝑥𝑖−c)(𝑥𝑖−c)⊤ of a leaf’s points gives the shape: the ellipsoid {𝑥:(𝑥−c)⊤Σ−1(𝑥−c)≤1} is long where the points spread. Scaling it by the largest value α that (𝑥𝑖−c)⊤Σ−1(𝑥𝑖−c) takes puts the farthest point exactly on the boundary:

T=Σ−1α,(𝑥−c)⊤T(𝑥−c)≤1.

4 Which leaf answers

A query 𝑥 is answered by the leaf

ν=arg min𝑖∈𝒞︀𝑥(𝑥−c𝑖)⊤T𝑖(𝑥−c𝑖)

among the κ centres nearest to it, and flagged if (𝑥−cν)⊤Tν(𝑥−cν)>1. The same rule in running text: ν is chosen among κ candidates.
